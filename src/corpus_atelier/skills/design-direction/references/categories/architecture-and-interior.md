# Architecture and interior

Adapted from the architecture-and-interior slice of the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- Space type and function: living room, gallery atrium, office, street facade.
- Camera height and lens: eye level, one-point, two-point, or elevated.
- Material palette: stone, concrete, timber, glass, textile, metal.
- Light direction and time; whether daylight, artificial, or mixed.
- Occupancy: empty staged space, or people implying scale.

## Structure that carries the message

- Fix the perspective before furnishing; keep verticals straight unless distortion is intended.
- Establish scale with doors, furniture, or human figures.
- Keep one light logic: window direction, bounce, and contact shadows must agree.
- Let materials read separately: roughness, reflection, and joint lines.
- Protect negative space; empty air is part of the composition.

## Common failures

- Fisheye or tilted verticals without intent.
- Floating furniture, impossible stairs, or non-structural openings.
- Light from two contradictory directions.
- Materials that merge into one grey surface.
- Missing scale cues, making the space feel like a model.

## Visual QA

- Check vertical convergence and horizon height.
- Trace material continuity across adjacent surfaces.
- Verify shadow direction and softness match the light source.
- Confirm scale cues are present and coherent.
- Check that no impossible structure appears.

## Optional style vocabulary

Architectural render, interior editorial photography, brutalist documentation, Japanese minimalism,
or a biophilic scheme when the brief supports it.
