# Infographics and field guides

Adapted from the infographics-and-field-guides slice of the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The artifact type: wall chart, field-guide card, museum board, explainer panel.
- The fixed regions and their order: title band, main diagram, detail insets, legend.
- The annotation system: lead lines, numbered labels, or lettered callouts.
- The label inventory, and whether numbers come from the brief or are illustrative only.

## Structure that carries the message

- Name the artifact type and the layout zones before any subject detail.
- Keep one label typography; size labels for the intended reading distance.
- Use a legend whenever more than two visual categories appear.
- Separate the figure from its ground with a clean panel or white field.
- Keep callouts short and anchored; a long annotation is a paragraph in disguise.

## Common failures

- A generic poster with icons rather than an information structure.
- Unlabeled diagrams and missing legends.
- Invented statistics, dates, or taxonomic facts.
- Annotation crowding that hides the object being explained.
- Decorative texture behind label text.

## Visual QA

- Spell every label and compare numbering order with the callouts.
- Confirm the legend covers all visual categories.
- Check label size against the intended viewing distance.
- Verify figure-ground separation and that no label sits on busy texture.
- Confirm no fact appears without support in the brief.

## Optional style vocabulary

Museum board, scientific plate, editorial explainer, risograph infographic, or a flat vector
explainer with no historical label.
