# Rhetoric-led visual-language space

For a rhetoric-led objective, the governing question is what the audience should recognize,
believe, and do. Communication purpose comes first: decide the sign and the warrant, then the
visual language that carries them. Choose the visual language along the six dimensions below and
state observable decisions rather than family names.

Combine dimensions under the rules in the style-space index: one primary language, at most two
supporting techniques, contradictions resolved rather than averaged.

## 1. Sign carrier and warrant

What kind of thing is doing the communicating, and why should it be believed.

- verbal: the claim is carried by letterform, wording, and typographic structure alone;
- evidential: a photograph, document, object, or trace is offered as proof that the situation
  exists;
- diagrammatic: a relation, process, or comparison is drawn so that the inference is the message;
- emblematic: one concentrated sign or visual analogy stands for the claim, and its meaning depends
  on a stated resemblance or convention;
- enacted: a depicted scene lets the audience infer the claim from characters, objects, and events;
- indexical: a mark, trace, or cause-effect link points at the claim without depicting it.

Decide: which carrier leads, and what makes it credible to this audience. A solution that stacks
verbal, evidential, and emblematic carriers at equal strength has no argument structure.

## 2. Evidence and credibility signals

How the artifact earns trust, which is a visual decision, not a tone of voice.

- unretouched material: the surface shows use, wear, imperfect light, or a real location;
- staged precision: controlled light, aligned geometry, and flawless surfaces, signalling authority
  or manufacture;
- attributed material: a quotation, annotation, signature, stamp, or date makes a source visible;
- measured material: axes, units, legends, and honest scale, so the reader can check the claim;
- declared incompleteness: visible gaps, redactions, or a stated uncertainty, used deliberately;
- pre-authorized form: a format the audience already trusts, such as a form, a ticket, a specimen
  sheet, or a specification.

Decide: the credibility mechanism, and keep it consistent. A documentary claim rendered with
studio-perfect staging contradicts itself.

## 3. Hierarchy and reading path

How the audience is made to arrive at the message in the intended order.

- a single dominant element, with everything else subordinated by scale or isolation;
- a stated two-level jump, where the first reading and the second reading cannot be confused;
- a reading sequence, where numbered steps, panels, or a directional flow fix the order;
- a contrast pair, where the claim appears as the difference between two visible states;
- repetition with one variation, where the deviation is the message;
- compression, where density and proximity produce urgency;
- a declared resting zone, so the eye has somewhere to stop before the next action.

Decide: what the eye meets first, second, and third, and the visual means that produce that order.
State the means — scale, isolation, contrast, position, sharpness — not "clear hierarchy".

## 4. Semantic distance and ambiguity control

How literal the visual statement is, and where a wrong reading could arise.

- direct denotation: the depicted thing is the message;
- analogy: a stated resemblance carries the claim, and the shared attribute is named;
- metaphor: a substitution carries the claim, and the ground of the substitution stays visible;
- irony or reversal: an expected form is contradicted, and the contradiction is unambiguous;
- defamiliarization: a familiar subject is made strange so it is re-examined;
- deliberate openness: a controlled ambiguity that the audience is meant to resolve.

Decide: the distance, and the one plausible misreading being blocked. Ambiguity is allowed only
where the audience can resolve it; elsewhere it is a defect, not a device.

## 5. Material and surface as signal

Physical and production choices carry meaning and must agree with the warrant.

- uncoated stock, risograph, or newsprint: accessibility, immediacy, lower authority;
- coated stock or precise flat areas: control, manufacture, premium claim;
- screen-native construction: emission, motion, small-size reading, dark surroundings;
- hand-made surfaces: testimony, care, the individual voice;
- industrial or utilitarian material: system, compliance, function;
- reproduction artifacts used consciously: photocopy degradation, misregistration, compression.

Decide: the material claim, and keep the rendering consistent with it. A premium claim printed on
nonexistent cheap stock reads as a contradiction.

## 6. Density, rhythm, and tone

How much is present, how fast it moves, and how loud it is.

- sparse: few elements, wide margins, slow reading, and a claim that can afford silence;
- dense: many elements, small type, tight intervals, and a reader expected to scan;
- metrical: an even beat that supports a repeated or ritual address;
- syncopated: an uneven beat that interrupts habit and draws attention to a specific element;
- declarative: large, flat, frontal, and addressed outward;
- intimate: small scale, close framing, and a voice addressed to one reader.

Decide: the density and the beat, and match them to the intended action. Urgency and quiet spacing
cannot both be the strategy.

## Reading the dimensions as a whole

Re-read the six decisions as one act of communication. Confirm that the carrier, the credibility
signal, the reading path, and the tone all support the same claim, and that the material and
surface do not undercut it. Name the intended reading in one sentence; if the decisions do not
produce that sentence, one of them is wrong.

Then check that the chosen sign relations survive close inspection: any visual metaphor must be
explainable in terms of a stated shared attribute, and any borrowed form must keep the convention
the audience relies on.
