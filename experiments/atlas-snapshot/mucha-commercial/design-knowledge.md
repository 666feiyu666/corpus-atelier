# Source description

Alphonse Mucha's JOB poster is organized around a close-cropped female profile. Flowing hair,
smoke, oversized lettering, and a repeated geometric border form one integrated commercial
image. The palette combines violet, yellow ochre, pale green, cream, and black in flat printed
fields with strong contour lines.

The cigarette paper is not presented as an isolated package. Its commercial meaning is
established through the woman's smoking gesture and through the relationship between her figure
and the JOB lettering. The woman supplies the image's visual and emotional presence, while the
gesture and brand name make the product legible.

# Transferable design knowledge

## Relational product meaning

A product's prominence does not have to come from mechanical enlargement or isolation. In the
source, the advertised product becomes intelligible through a relationship among use, figure,
gesture, and brand lettering.

## Lettering as compositional structure

Display lettering can participate in the composition rather than appearing as a separate label.
Overlap, interruption, enclosure, repetition, alignment, and separation are possible relationships
between lettering and other visual elements, not requirements.

## Contrast between formal systems

The source uses flowing organic lines against a repeated geometric enclosure. More generally,
contrast between formal systems can organize movement, establish hierarchy, or stabilize a dense
composition.

# Transfer boundaries

Transfer relationships and design principles, not source-specific motifs.

Do not treat the source's female figure, product-use gesture, close crop, oversized lettering,
geometric border, or palette as defaults for an unknown future brief.
