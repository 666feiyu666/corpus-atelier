# Photography and cinematic

Adapted from the photography, cinematic-and-animation, and cinematic-film-reference slices of the
GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery (https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed
and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The capture frame: casual phone snapshot, editorial portrait, cinema still, archival photo.
- One camera position and lens feel, not several.
- Light direction and time of day; whether the source is visible.
- Styling and set: what is actually in the frame, and what is deliberately absent.
- Whether a film reference supplies grammar (framing, palette, light) rather than content.

## Structure that carries the message

- Choose one dominant capture frame and keep it consistent through the description.
- Separate foreground, middle ground, and background; let atmosphere do the depth work.
- Include ordinary imperfection: grain, slight motion, uneven light, real surfaces.
- Keep shadow direction, reflections, and highlights agreeing with the stated source.
- Keep anatomy plausible: hands, fingers, reflections, and contact points.

## Common failures

- Over-polished, plastic-looking surfaces.
- Conflicting camera specifications that cannot coexist.
- Impossible reflections or shadows pointing two ways.
- Extra fingers, fused limbs, or mismatched eye direction.
- Copying a named film's characters or scenes instead of its grammar.

## Visual QA

- Inspect hands, faces, and contact points at full resolution.
- Check shadow and reflection agreement.
- Confirm grain, depth of field, and motion are consistent.
- Confirm the frame matches one stated camera position.
- Confirm no real-person likeness or unapproved logo appears.

## Optional style vocabulary

Documentary 35 mm, editorial portrait, neo-noir, 1970s film, long-exposure night, or a
contemporary natural-light look with no movement label.
