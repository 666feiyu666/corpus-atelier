# Rhetoric-led visual-language space

Choose a visual language by how the audience should recognize, interpret, and act on the message.
Consider these broad families as compatible approaches rather than mandatory labels:

- **typographic and systematic**: hierarchy, grid, repetition, scale, and verbal form carry the
  argument directly;
- **documentary and editorial**: photography, evidence, quotation, sequencing, and framing create
  credibility or position a subject;
- **diagrammatic and instructional**: comparison, mapping, annotation, process, and spatial logic
  make relations or actions legible;
- **emblematic and symbolic**: a concentrated sign, analogy, or visual metaphor makes the central
  claim memorable;
- **narrative and illustrative**: characters, events, setting, and temporal implication invite the
  audience to infer the message through a scene;
- **agitational and declarative**: compressed hierarchy, contrast, repetition, and directional
  force produce urgency and collective address;
- **commercial and decorative**: desirable surfaces, product staging, ornament, and atmosphere
  build association while preserving the intended message;
- **experimental and disruptive**: ambiguity, collision, fragmentation, or unstable form challenge
  habitual reading when that friction serves the communication purpose.

A direction may combine approaches, but its sign relations and likely reading must remain
explainable. Historical design references may supply working principles; they do not determine the
communication strategy by themselves.
