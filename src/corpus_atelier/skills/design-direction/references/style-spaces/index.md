# Style spaces

A visual language is a set of observable making decisions, not a name. This index holds the rules
that apply to every style space; the objective space supplies the dimensions you choose within.

Use this index with exactly one objective space. It does not list styles, and no entry here may be
used as a label.

## Choose by dimension, not by family

- Work through the objective space dimension by dimension. For each dimension, decide what this
  artifact needs before you pick wording.
- State each choice as the observable decision that produces it — a contour behavior, a value
  structure, a palette limit, a mark. If a reader could not verify the choice by looking at the
  finished image, it is not a decision yet.
- Never start from a familiar look and justify it afterwards. The category requirements and the
  design objective produce the choices; the wording describes them.

## One primary language, at most two supporting techniques

- The primary language sets the medium, the contour and modeling logic, and the value structure.
  It controls the image.
- Supporting techniques may add color behavior, surface texture, structural treatment, or a
  post-treatment. Two is the ceiling in almost every direction; one is usually enough.
- A supporting technique that would contradict the primary language's medium, contour, or value
  logic is not support. Drop it rather than splitting the image between two systems.
- Do not stack compatible-sounding phrases. Three adjectives describing the same surface decision
  are one decision written three times.

## Resolve conflicts explicitly

Decisions from different dimensions can contradict each other. When they do, keep the side that
carries the governing idea and remove the other; do not ask the renderer to average them. Recurring
contradictions:

- flat, hard-edged vector surfaces combined with heavy impasto or painterly buildup;
- no-outline treatment combined with thick closed black contour;
- hard cel-banded values combined with continuous watercolor bleeding;
- minimal, low-detail structure combined with photoreal detail across the whole frame;
- matte paper or uncoated stock combined with strong plastic specular highlights;
- loose hand-drawn edges combined with mechanically uniform geometric line weight;
- documentary credibility claimed while the framing, lighting, and staging are studio-perfect;
- a compressed, urgent hierarchy combined with evenly weighted, quiet spacing.

## Emit the choice as decisions

Every chosen dimension that carries the direction must appear in `direction_decisions` as one row:

- `visual_language` for the primary language and the overall construction principle;
- `image_making` for medium, rendering logic, contour, value, texture, and detail distribution;
- `palette` for the color decision, including its limits;
- `composition` for space, structure, and reading order;
- `typography` when letterform behavior is part of the language;
- `concept` when the language exists to carry a claim rather than an experience.

Name the decision, not the dimension. "Contour is a single unmodulated hairline, with all volume
carried by flat fill" is a decision; "line work" is a category.

## Differentiate the portfolio on these dimensions

Directions in one portfolio must be materially different, not differently worded. Any two
directions should differ on at least two dimensions, and at least one of those differences must be
visible in the silhouette or the value structure — not only in surface texture or palette
temperature. If two directions differ only by adjectives, one of them is not a direction.

## When a style name is legitimate

- A name is allowed only when the decisions you already made are what that name denotes, and the
  name adds information for the reader. It is a summary of decisions, never a substitute for them.
- If the brief names a style or movement, treat it as a shared invariant across the portfolio and
  vary the remaining dimensions.
- If a cultural, vernacular, contemporary, or medium-led language outside the dimensions is the
  right answer, use it and describe it by its observable decisions. The dimensions are a coverage
  check, not a closed set.

## Division of labour

- The category guide decides what this kind of artifact must get right. It does not choose the
  language.
- The objective space provides the dimensions in which language is chosen.
- The design craft checklist checks the resulting direction: hierarchy, separability of controls,
  copy structure, physical coherence.
