# Technical and scientific

Adapted from the technical-illustration and scientific-and-educational slices of the
GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery (https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed
and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The illustration type: exploded view, cutaway, cross-section, assembly plate, anatomy chart.
- The component inventory and its order.
- The callout system: numbered labels, lead lines, or a parts list.
- The style boundary: blueprint, engineering plate, museum chart, editorial explainer.
- Which facts are given. Anything else stays schematic and visibly unmeasured.

## Structure that carries the message

- Fix the projection (isometric, dimetric, orthographic) and keep every part on it.
- Space exploded parts by a consistent gap along one axis.
- Number callouts in reading order and keep the label list in the same order.
- Differentiate materials by surface and edge treatment, not by color alone.
- Keep the background neutral so lines and labels stay legible.

## Common failures

- Mechanically impossible assemblies or parts that cannot connect.
- Unlabeled components, or labels that point nowhere.
- Mixed projections inside one figure.
- Decorative clutter competing with the diagram.
- Invented measurements presented as specification.

## Visual QA

- Count parts and confirm the callout numbers match the list.
- Trace each label to its part.
- Check projection consistency across the whole figure.
- Confirm materials are distinguishable without color.
- Confirm no invented specification or measurement.

## Optional style vocabulary

Blueprint, engineering plate, cutaway render, museum chart, or a clean editorial explainer with no
historical label.
