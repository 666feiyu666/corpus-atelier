# User brief template

Describe the communication problem in your own words. Short answers are enough.
You may use this template or provide a free-form request. Leave a field blank or
write "open to proposal" when you want the designer to suggest an answer.
You do not need to choose a visual concept, motif, color palette, or sign category.

## Core brief

### 1. Purpose

Why is this poster needed? What would you like the intended reader to understand,
feel, reconsider, or do after seeing it?

### 2. Core message

What is the main idea the reader should take away? Express it in ordinary language.
This is the meaning to communicate, not necessarily the final headline or visible copy.
If several messages matter, identify the most important one.

view it on a phone, or spend time reading it? Note viewing distance, competing visual
information, or other conditions that matter, if known.

## Brief example

- **Purpose:** Invite people who feel pressure to remain productive to consider a short pause.
- **Core message:** Rest does not have to be earned through productivity.
- **Audience:** Students and office workers who recognize that pressure.
- **Context:** A shared indoor noticeboard, usually seen briefly while passing.
- **Deliverable:** A portrait poster in English; dimensions are open to proposal.
- **Required content and sources:** No exact copy or source imagery supplied. Propose the wording.
- **Hard constraints:** Do not make unsupported health claims.
- **Preferences:** An inviting tone, without blame. Visual direction is open.
- **Missing information:** Proceed with stated assumptions unless an essential decision needs clarification.

This example illustrates the template; it does not supply defaults for other briefs.
