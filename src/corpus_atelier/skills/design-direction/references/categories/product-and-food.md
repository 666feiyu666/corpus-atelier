# Product and food

Adapted from the product-and-food slice of the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The product's real geometry and label: shape, cap, seams, material, exact pack copy.
- Whether the scene is studio, table-top, or in use.
- Light direction and surface: softbox, window light, hard sun, reflective table.
- Which single prop or ingredient earns its place.

## Structure that carries the message

- Place the hero product first; hero framing comes from camera height and crop, not from size alone.
- Keep contact: shadow, reflection, condensation, or a hand that explains scale.
- Separate material, light, and palette statements instead of compressing them into "premium".
- Keep label copy exact and legible at the intended size.
- Reserve an area for price, badge, or campaign copy when the brief requires it.

## Common failures

- Warped or impossible packaging geometry.
- Misspelled or invented label copy and fake brand marks.
- Product floating without contact or shadow.
- Plastic CGI sheen where the brief asks for real material.
- Splash or ingredient chaos that hides the product.

## Visual QA

- Read the pack copy exactly.
- Check silhouette and geometry against the real product.
- Confirm contact shadow, reflection, and material continuity.
- Check the light direction is consistent across product, props, and background.
- Confirm no unapproved logo or claim appears.

## Style vocabulary

None is supplied here. This guide sets what this kind of artifact must get right, not how it looks.
Choose the visual language in the observable dimensions of the style space, and state each choice as
the decision that produces it rather than as a label.
