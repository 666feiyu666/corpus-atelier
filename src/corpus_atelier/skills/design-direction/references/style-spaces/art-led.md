# Art-led visual-language space

For an art-led objective, the governing question is what the work should make a viewer perceive.
Choose the visual language along the six dimensions below. Read them as coverage checks: decide
each dimension this artifact depends on, and state the observable decision rather than a family
name. A dimension the work genuinely does not need may stay open.

Combine dimensions under the rules in the style-space index: one primary language, at most two
supporting techniques, contradictions resolved rather than averaged.

## 1. Medium and rendering logic

What physically makes the surface. This dimension is primary: it fixes what the other dimensions
may do.

- photographic capture: a lens produced the image; depth, focus, and exposure behave optically;
- lens-free rendering: three-dimensional geometry shaded by material response rather than by light
  recorded from a scene;
- drawn and painted: a wet or dry mark-making tool produced the surface, with the mark legible as
  such;
- printed reproduction: the image is built from a screening or impression process, and its artifacts
  are visible;
- assembled surfaces: cut, torn, pasted, stitched, or layered material joins are visible;
- vector construction: shapes are built from paths and fills, with no simulated physical surface;
- a hybrid where one medium visibly dominates and the other is applied in a bounded region.

Decide: which medium governs, and whether any second medium is present at all. An image that reads
as half photographic and half painted without a reason is unresolved.

## 2. Line, shape, and contour

How edges and forms are delimited.

- no contour at all: forms separate by value, color, or focus only;
- a single unmodulated hairline, uniform in weight everywhere;
- variable-weight contour that thickens where mass or pressure would;
- contour present only at overlaps, absent on the silhouette;
- hand-drawn contour with visible irregularity and overshoot;
- forms built from geometric primitives, so contour is implied by the construction;
- shape simplification with a stated limit: how far anatomy, foliage, or architecture is reduced,
  and what must survive that reduction.

Decide: whether contour exists, what it is made of, and how shape is simplified. Contour behavior
is the fastest way to make two directions visibly different.

## 3. Value and light

How volume and depth are produced.

- flat values with no modeled volume;
- two or three hard value steps, banded at a stated boundary;
- continuous tonal modeling across the form;
- strong directional light with a deep shadow side and a distinct core shadow;
- diffuse ambient light with low contrast and clearly separated local values;
- value carried by the medium's own behavior, such as ink pooling or pigment granulation;
- a deliberate value structure that decides where the darkest darks and lightest lights are
  allowed to sit.

Decide: the value structure and the light direction, once. Every element stays accountable to that
one light.

## 4. Color

How the palette is bounded and what it is doing.

- a stated count of colors, and which role each plays;
- a single hue family with a manufactured accent;
- a complementary or near-complementary opposition carrying the contrast;
- local color with light shifting temperature across the form;
- non-naturalistic color used to carry emotion or symbol;
- neutral structure with color confined to one region or one element;
- color produced by the medium, such as overprinted inks, transparent washes, or film response.

Decide: the color limit and the color logic. "Rich" or "vibrant" is not a color decision; a stated
count, hue relation, and temperature shift is.

## 5. Texture and detail distribution

Where the surface is dense and where it is quiet. Detail is a budget, not a quality level.

- uniform fine grain or tooth across the whole surface;
- material texture only where a material is present, clean edges elsewhere;
- visible process artifacts: brush loading, dry-brush breakup, halftone dots, registration offset,
  scratches, screen weave;
- detail concentrated on the focal element and deliberately withdrawn toward the edges;
- flat areas kept absolutely clean, so texture becomes an event;
- high-frequency detail across the frame, with no resting area.

Decide: the texture and the distribution of detail, including which region stays quiet. An even
detail level everywhere removes the hierarchy the direction depends on.

## 6. Space and composition logic

How the image is built in the plane.

- a declared depth structure: how many planes, and what separates them;
- flattened space, where overlapping is the only cue for near and far;
- one dominant mass against a large quiet field;
- full-bleed construction with the subject cropped by the frame;
- a single axis of movement or tilt, and what resists it;
- repetition or rhythm as the organizing method, with the count stated;
- a deliberately violated grid, where the violation carries the meaning.

Decide: the spatial logic and the first element the eye meets, with the means that produce that
order. Placement, local contrast, isolation, or scale — not "balanced".

## Reading the dimensions as a whole

After choosing, re-read the six decisions as one physical scene. Confirm that the light, material,
texture, and detail statements describe a single object made by a single process. If the medium is
drawn, no lighting may presume a lens. If the surface is matte, no highlight may behave like
polished plastic. Reconcile the contradiction by removing the weaker statement.

Then check coverage honestly: three dimensions may be enough for a restrained direction, and a
direction that touches all six weakly is worse than one that resolves three decisively.
