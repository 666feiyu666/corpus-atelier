# Art-led visual-language space

Choose a visual language by the experience the work should create, not by attaching a style name
after the concept is formed. Consider these broad families as compatible dimensions rather than
exclusive presets:

- **classical and idealized**: stable order, clarified contour, balanced proportion, restrained
  incident, and an elevated or timeless mood;
- **observational and realist**: specific people, places, objects, surfaces, and social conditions
  treated without decorative idealization;
- **sublime and romantic**: dramatic scale, charged atmosphere, strong light, movement, and
  emotional tension;
- **atmospheric and impressionistic**: transient light, optical color, open edges, immediacy, and
  the sensation of a passing moment;
- **constructed and decorative**: flattened or deliberately organized space, rhythmic contour,
  patterned surfaces, symbolic color, and strong pictorial structure;
- **expressive**: subjective distortion, non-naturalistic color, emphatic line, and marks that carry
  emotional pressure;
- **surreal and uncanny**: dream logic, impossible but precise relationships, estrangement, and
  shifts of scale, context, or identity;
- **abstract geometric**: reduction to proportion, interval, plane, line, and color relationships;
- **abstract gestural or material**: movement, accumulation, texture, process, and the physical
  behavior of the medium;
- **photographic and cinematic**: lens position, cropping, focus, exposure, sequence, staging, and
  light used as the primary expressive language;
- **collage, assemblage, and mixed media**: visible joins among heterogeneous fragments, materials,
  times, or representational systems.

A direction may combine families when their principles reinforce one coherent experience. It may
also use a culturally specific, vernacular, contemporary, corpus-derived, or medium-led language
outside this map. Name a historical movement only when its actual principles shape the work.
