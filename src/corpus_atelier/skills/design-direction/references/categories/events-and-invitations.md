# Events and invitations

Adapted from the events-and-experience and wayfinding work in the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The format: digital invitation, printed poster, ticket, or wayfinding sign.
- The facts that must be true: name, date, time, venue, admission, call to action.
- Tone: ceremonial, playful, urgent, intimate.
- Where a host or sponsor logo will be added later.

## Structure that carries the message

- Order the information: event name, then date and venue, then action or detail.
- Keep one clean information block; do not scatter facts across the image.
- For wayfinding, fix an icon and arrow system and repeat it identically.
- Leave a visibly empty band for a logo that will be added later.
- Let decoration support the facts, never compete with the date.

## Common failures

- Invented dates, venues, prices, or sponsors.
- Ornament covering the information block.
- Low-contrast date or venue that disappears at thumbnail size.
- Different icon styles on the same sign system.
- Handwritten imitation that turns Chinese or bilingual copy into gibberish.

## Visual QA

- Compare every fact against the brief, character by character.
- Read the date and venue at thumbnail size.
- Confirm no invented claims, prices, or sponsors.
- Check the reserved logo band is empty and the icon system is consistent.

## Style vocabulary

None is supplied here. This guide sets what this kind of artifact must get right, not how it looks.
Choose the visual language in the observable dimensions of the style space, and state each choice as
the decision that produces it rather than as a label.
