# Typography and posters

Adapted from the typography-and-posters slice of the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- Viewing distance and final size: a wall poster, a phone feed, or a printed A2 sheet.
- The complete copy inventory: headline, subhead, body, date or price, fine print.
- One dominant type voice and at most one supporting voice; whether the type is the image.
- Canvas ratio and margins before any surface detail.

## Structure that carries the message

- Reserve the layout zones first: title block, main visual, information band, quiet margin.
- Make the scale jump between hierarchy levels unmistakable; equal-weight blocks read as a banner.
- Align to a small grid; keep one optical axis and consistent margins.
- Place text on calm ground, not on busy texture; use a deliberate scrim or panel when needed.
- If a logo area is reserved, leave it visibly empty rather than filling it.

## Common failures

- Every text block at the same weight, so nothing leads.
- Invented sponsor strips, fake small print, or pseudo-text outside the approved copy.
- Garbled or warped characters in dense Chinese or bilingual layouts.
- Centered, evenly spaced mush with no reading path.
- Decorative ornaments competing with the headline.

## Visual QA

- Read every approved string character by character.
- Squint or shrink to thumbnail: the headline must still win.
- Check legibility at the stated distance and the smallest printed size.
- Confirm margins, baseline alignment, and the reserved empty band.

## Optional style vocabulary

Typographic discipline, geometric luxury, diagonal force, vibrating lettering, or a contemporary
editorial voice. Use one bounded anchor, not a stack of labels.
