# Editorial and covers

Adapted from the magazine, editorial, and cover work in the GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery
(https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- The reading surface: feed thumbnail, article header, print cover, album or podcast tile.
- The exact title and any standfirst; whether the image illustrates the title or argues with it.
- One governing idea for the cover, not a summary of the whole text.
- Which crops must survive: platform circle crop, banner crop, square tile.

## Structure that carries the message

- One dominant visual plus a title block. A cover with three ideas reads as none.
- Keep the title clear of the busiest texture and away from crops that will be cut.
- Reserve a quiet corner when an avatar, play button, or platform badge will overlay it.
- For album art, keep the square composition intact at thumbnail size.

## Common failures

- Literal stock-photo illustration of the headline.
- Title hidden in texture, or set in a corner the platform crop removes.
- Several competing metaphors.
- Fake issue numbers, barcodes, or contributor names that were never approved.
- Type that only works at full size.

## Visual QA

- Shrink to roughly 200 px wide: is the idea still readable?
- Check the platform crop, especially a centered circle.
- Read the title and standfirst exactly; confirm no extra words.
- Confirm the quiet corner is actually quiet.

## Style vocabulary

None is supplied here. This guide sets what this kind of artifact must get right, not how it looks.
Choose the visual language in the observable dimensions of the style space, and state each choice as
the decision that produces it rather than as a label.
