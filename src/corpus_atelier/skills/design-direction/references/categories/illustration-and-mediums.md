# Illustration and mediums

Adapted from the illustration, watercolor, ink, pixel-art, isometric, and papercut slices of the
GPT-Image2-Skill reference gallery (https://github.com/wuyoscar/GPT-Image2-Skill, MIT). Condensed
and rewritten for this pipeline.

## Decide before rendering

- One medium: watercolor wash, ink, gouache, flat vector, risograph, pixel art, papercut, low-poly.
- Shape language and line weight; whether outlines exist at all.
- Palette limits: how many inks or tones the medium plausibly allows.
- How the medium behaves at edges, overlaps, and in shadow.

## Structure that carries the message

- Build one readable silhouette before detail.
- Keep a single light logic even in a flat or decorative treatment.
- Let the medium's own behavior carry texture: wash blooms, dry brush, halftone dots, pixel grid.
- Keep the background in the same medium as the subject unless collage separation is intended.
- Match the level of realism across all elements of the same scene.

## Common failures

- Photographic rendering leaking into a flat or drawn treatment.
- Mixed media without an intended joins-and-layers logic.
- Texture noise overwhelming form.
- Pixel work off the grid, or line work with inconsistent weight.
- A palette larger than the stated medium allows.

## Visual QA

- Confirm one medium across subject, props, and background.
- Check edge behavior and texture at full resolution.
- Count inks or tones against the stated limit.
- Squint: the silhouette must still read.
- Confirm no unintended photorealism or extra detail.

## Style vocabulary

None is supplied here. This guide sets what this kind of artifact must get right, not how it looks.
Choose the visual language in the observable dimensions of the style space, and state each choice as
the decision that produces it rather than as a label.
